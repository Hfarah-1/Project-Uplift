Europe Approves Bionic Eye Implant That Gives Sight Back to the Blind

XL
Xander Lundblad
September 5, 2026
3 min read

A breakthrough innovation has passed clinical trials and has been approved for treatment in Europe, one that allows people suffering from age-related visual decline partial reversal in their condition.

The innovation being rolled out this month commercially is PRIMA, a retinal implant installed through patients' eyes. PRIMA turns images into electric signals that stimulate retina nerve cells.

After the implant is placed, PRIMA comes with glasses that process images and turn them into near infrared light. This light is then sent to the implant, stimulating the retina through electrical signals and helping the brain produce images, all while avoiding interference with natural vision.

PRIMA's introduction in Europe offers potentially groundbreaking visual improvements. Around 2.2 billion people worldwide suffer from vision loss or impairment, and 80% of this decline occurs after the age of 50. Out of these 2.2 billion, around five million have geographic atrophy. Geographic atrophy is the ocular condition where photoreceptors in the center of the retina are destroyed, leaving it impossible to read or recognize faces.

Test results with PRIMA have already shown unprecedented visual restoration. A clinical trial published in the New England Journal of Medicine found that 32 participants who used the chip for a year gained an average of 25.5 letters on the standard eye chart used in ocular exams.

While the device currently produces just black and white vision through a narrow field, PRIMA's developers are working on attempts to produce models with color vision and higher acuity. 20-40 devices are expected for installation in 2026, with a goal set for up to 200 across the globe in 2027 as PRIMA pends approval in the United States. PRIMA's novelty advancement paves the way for further breakthrough visual innovations and exciting developments in the neurotech industry.
